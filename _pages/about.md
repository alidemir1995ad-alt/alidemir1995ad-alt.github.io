My research interests lie in applied microeconomics, particularly **labor and demographic economics**, **migration**, and **urban and regional economics**.

My job market paper, [**German Internal Migration: A Marriage Market Perspective**](/files/Demir_JMP.pdf), investigates whether the large wave of young East–West internal migration after German reunification altered marriage formation and marital stability across receiving West German districts. I assemble a newly digitized, boundary-harmonized panel of court-ordered divorces (extending back to 1980), marriages, births, and regional household income accounts covering 320 West German districts, paired with a pre-Berlin Wall (1961 census) shift-share instrument. I find that greater migration exposure significantly lowered local divorce rates without increasing marriage formation or asymmetrically shifting wife- versus husband-filed divorce petitions—evidence that rules out thicker partner-market and sex-ratio bargaining channels in favor of household transition costs and residential sorting. 

My broader [research](/research/) agenda links German household panel data (SOEP) with high-resolution housing-market data (RWI-GEO-REDX) to study how housing security, local rents, and regional disparities shape family expansion and the transition to adulthood.

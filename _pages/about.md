I am an applied microeconomist working on **migration, housing and family formation**. I completed my PhD in Economics at **Ruhr University Bochum in October 2026**, supervised by Thomas K. Bauer and Arnaud Chevalier. My primary fields are labor and demographic economics, with secondary interests in urban and regional economics.

My job market paper, [**German Internal Migration: A Marriage Market Perspective**](/job-market-paper/), studies how post-reunification East–West migration affected marriage and divorce in receiving districts. I assemble a newly digitized panel of **320 West German districts over 1985–2007**, extending back to 1980 for a subset, and use settlement ties recorded in the 1961 census to predict migration exposure. Divorce fell persistently from the mid-1990s in districts with stronger historical ties, while marriages did not rise. This pattern is inconsistent with the paper's thicker-partner-market prediction and is consistent with higher costs of forming and dissolving households, although the data do not directly identify that mechanism.

My other [research](/research/) uses the German Socio-Economic Panel (SOEP) and local housing-market data (RWI-GEO-REDX) to study homeownership and the transition to a second birth, and local rents and young adults' departure from the parental home.

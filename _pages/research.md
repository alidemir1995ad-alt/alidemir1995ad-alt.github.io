My research is in **labor and demographic economics**, particularly migration, fertility and family formation, and **urban and regional economics**, with a focus on housing. My current papers combine original historical district data, the German Socio-Economic Panel (SOEP-Core), and local housing-market data to study family transitions in Germany.

## Job Market Paper

### German Internal Migration: A Marriage Market Perspective

Did post-reunification East–West migration change marriage formation and marital stability in receiving West German districts? I assemble a newly digitized, harmonized panel of 320 districts over 1985–2007, extending to 1980 for a subset. A shift-share instrument combines districts' 1961 East-linked settlement shares with young-adult arrival rates in other West German states.

Divorce fell persistently from the mid-1990s in districts with stronger historical ties: the decline in the top relative to the bottom quintile of the share was about **14 percent of the mean**. Event studies extending to 1980 do not reject parallel pre-reunification trends, and a placebo settlement share without East German ties predicts neither arrivals nor divorce. The preferred IV estimate corresponds to about **9 percent of the mean per standard deviation of measured migration exposure**; it scales the reduced form by post-1991 gross arrivals rather than identifying a constant marginal effect per arrival.

Marriages did not rise, and the analysis detects no statistically significant difference between wife- and husband-filed divorce responses. The pattern is inconsistent with a thicker partner market and is consistent with higher household formation and dissolution costs, although the district data do not directly identify that mechanism.

## Working Papers

### Housing Security and Family Expansion: Evidence from German Homeownership

Using SOEP-Core, I study homeownership and fertility among West German women with district and year fixed effects and parental homeownership as an instrument. Homeownership accelerates the transition from first to second birth, while the effect on the cumulative number of children is small and statistically insignificant, consistent with a **tempo effect**. Effects on completed family size remain open. Estimates are local to women whose ownership depends on their parents' ownership, and inference is robust to weak instruments.

### Transition to Adulthood: Local Rents and Leaving the Parental Home

I link SOEP-Core residence histories to RWI-GEO-REDX quality-adjusted rent indices at the parental-home postal code to study young adults' first exit from the parental home, with heterogeneity by migration background. Across locations, higher rents predict earlier exit, consistent with high-rent areas being opportunity-rich. Within postal codes over time, the rent gradient turns weakly negative and imprecise. Bounds rule out large affordability effects, while lead–lag placebo tests attribute part of the remaining gradient to local trends rather than rents. These patterns distinguish spatial differences from changes within places; they do not establish a causal effect of rents on exit timing.
